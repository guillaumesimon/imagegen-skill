16-bit pixel art in the house style of early-90s Sega Mega Drive games.

Chunky, clearly visible square pixels with hard edges and no anti-aliasing. A
tight palette — around sixty saturated colours on screen, not more — with
shading built from cross-hatch and checkerboard dithering instead of gradients.
A dark outline around every sprite. Flat, deliberately limited colour ramps:
three or four steps per material, never a smooth blend.

Low native resolution, the kind of image that would sit inside a 320×224 frame.
Strong contrast, black used as a real colour rather than an absence of light.
Backgrounds simplified into flat bands and repeating tiled patterns.

Avoid: smooth modern shading, soft light, blur, anti-aliased edges, photographic
depth of field, gradients.
